# Optimization under Persistent State-Dependent Bias: Gradient-based Method and Complexity Analysis 

Zhaoxian Wu · Quan Xiao · Tayfun Gokmen · Tianyi Chen Abstract This paper studies the convergence of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) when the imple-mented updates are subject to a persistent and state-dependent bias, in which the desired update is scaled by response functions component-wise. Our first contribution is to demonstrate that SGD in this setting implicitly optimizes a penalized problem whose minimizer does not coincide with the true minimizer. To mitigate this convergence failure, we reformulate the original task as an equivalent bilevel optimization problem and propose a gradient-based algorithm, termed Residual Learning . Theoretical analysis shows that Residual Learning finds a solution to the original, unbiased optimization problem despite the hardware imperfections. Beyond exact convergence, we quantify how the response functions affect convergence complexity via the hardware condition number and show that a polynomial depen-dence on it is unavoidable in general, via a construction of a hard instance. The theoretical results are supported by numerical simulations that demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. 

Keywords Stochastic optimization; bilevel optimization; approximate computing; in-situ optimization

1.1 Motivated application: Analog-based physical computing 

This computational model is motivated by analog computing for optimization problems, which arises from the gap between the computing requirements of large AI models and the limited hardware development. Modern hardware accelerators, such as GPUs and TPUs (Jouppi et al., 2023), are usually 

digital computing hardware , in which frequent data movement is involved in computation. To further improve computational efficiency, an analog computing paradigm has been proposed (Haensch et al., 2019; Sebastian et al., 2020), wherein the optimization variables are represented by continuous physical quantities (e.g., conductance states of resistive elements). This novel computing approach leverages Kirchhoff’s and Ohm’s laws to accelerate matrix-vector multiplication, a bottleneck in AI computing. Without moving variables during optimization processes, analog computing hardware achieves 10 ×-10,000 × energy efficiency than GPU (Jain et al., 2019; Cosemans et al., 2019; Papistas et al., 2021) in the inference, and 1, 000 × in the training (Wang et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2020; Nandakumar et al., 2020). Unlike digital hardware, analog hardware updates variables in-situ by applying electrical pulses to resistive elements. Since the resistive element responds to positive and negative pulses differently, the desired update in ∆z is scaled by q+( · ) or q−( · ) depending on its sign. Empirical evidence shows that the response is state-dependent and asymmetric, which can be captured by the dynamics (3) (Burr et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2015; Wu et al., 2024). 1.2 Main results 

This paper aims to establish a rigorous optimization foundation under the assumption that all variables follow the dynamics (4) . A primary challenge is the asymmetric update . That is, for most of z, the response to positive and negative stimuli is different, i.e., q+(z)̸ = q−(z). Therefore, intended positive and negative updates of equal magnitude produce unequal changes in the variable. Specifically, a point 

w⋄ satisfies q+(w⋄) = q−(w⋄), we say w⋄ is a symmetric point . Except for the symmetric point, the update is asymmetric elsewhere. As a stepping stone, we first answer the following question: 

Q1) What is the impact of the persistent state-dependent bias on the optimization? 

The study begins by investigating its impact on SGD. We show that the stationary point of Analog SGD 

is not the minimizer of f ( · ) but an approximate minimizer ˜W ∗ of a penalized objective f (W ) + φ(W ),where φ(W ) is an implicit penalty that will be defined in Theorem 1. In other words, instead of optimizing problem P, Analog SGD optimizes another penalized problem, called problem IP . We show that the penalty coefficient is proportional to the noise level of the stochastic gradients, and the minimizers of φ(W ) are the symmetric point W ⋄ ∈ RD , which is obtained by replicating w⋄ D times. Since W ⋄̸ = W ∗, Wk can not converge to W ∗, and f (Wk) does not converge to f ∗; see §2. It motivates us to ask the following question:

1.3 Literature review 

We review prior work from two aspects: optimization via biased gradient oracles and bilevel optimization. 

Optimization via biased gradient oracles. Digital SGD , equipped with an unbiased gradient oracle, has become a standard method for solving the problem P. However, errors in gradient calculations may appear deterministically in various contexts. For example, in bilevel optimization, the problem typically involves an inner-loop or approximation sequence to compute hyper-gradients (Arbel and Mairal, 2022a; Chen et al., 2021); in distributed optimization, gradients can be compressed or quantized to reduce communication (Alistarh et al., 2017; Magnússon et al., 2020; Stich et al., 2018; Wangni et al., 2018; Safaryan et al., 2022). Without any special structure, general biased gradient oracles typically incur asymptotic error and lead to inexact convergence. For example, consider a general 

biased SGD recursion Wk+1 = Wk − α(∇f (Wk; ξk) + b(Wk)) with the assumption that there exist constants 0 ≤ A1 < 1, A 2 ≥ 0 such that ∥b(W )∥2 ≤ A1∥∇ f (W )∥2 + A2, biased SGD (Ajalloeian and Stich, 2020) has non-vanishing asymptotic error O(A2/(1 − A1)) if A2̸ = 0 . In some applications, the bounds of the asymptotic error are not artifacts and are even tight; e.g, in the distributed computing system, the gradient estimation inevitably becomes biased if part of the workers become unreliable (Wu et al., 2020; Karimireddy et al., 2022). A crucial insight in modern stochastic optimization, however, is that bias is not inherently incompatible with convergence. For example, in finite-sum optimization, several techniques can be incorporated into SGD to improve the convergence performance, such as without-replacement sampling orders (Haochen and Sra, 2019; Gurbuzbalaban et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2021) or variance reduction (Nguyen et al., 2017; Schmidt et al., 2017; Cutkosky and Orabona, 2019). In these methods, even though biased gradient oracles are employed, the gradient oracles are asymptotically unbiased , i.e., the induced bias vanishes as the variables converge to the solution. In general, Khanh et al. (2024) provides a condition under which biased SGD converges exactly. If asymptotic unbiasedness does not hold, one approach is to introduce auxiliary variables to mitigate bias during optimization, such as error feedback (Karimireddy et al., 2019). Unlike existing work, which assumes ideal updates to auxiliary variables, this paper considers a more challenging setting in which any auxiliary variable suffers from the biased update (4). Gradient-based methods for bilevel optimization. Bilevel optimization has a long history in optimization that dates back to (Bracken and McGill, 1973; Ye and Zhu, 1995; Vicente and Calamai, 1994; Colson et al., 2007), where the upper-level objective depends on the optimal solution of the lower-level problem. Recent theoretical works have focused on developing efficient gradient-based bilevel methods with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees (Ghadimi and Wang, 2018; Ji et al., 2021; Hong et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2021). Since the gradient of the bilevel objective depends on the Hessian inverse of the lower-level objective, existing literature proposed different Hessian inversion approximation methods including unrolling differentiation (Franceschi et al., 2017, 2018; Grazzi et al., 2020; Shaban et al., 2019), implicit differentiation (Chen et al., 2021; Ghadimi and Wang, 2018; Hong et al., 2023; Pedregosa, 2016; Khanduri et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022), conjugate gradient (Ji et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2021) and its warm-started single-loop versions (Arbel and Mairal, 2022a; Li et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2023; Arbel and Mairal, 2022b). All of the above approximations are based on second-order information. In addition, researchers have proposed first-order methods, such as equilibrium backpropagation (Scellier and Bengio, 2017; Scellier, 2021; Zucchet and Sacramento, 2022) and penalty approach (Shen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2022; Kwon et al., 2023, 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Lu and Mei, 2023; Jiang et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2024; Mehra and Hamm, 2021). However, even when the lower-level problem is strongly convex, directly applying the above methods typically results in only stationary convergence, due to distortions caused by the nested structure. In fact, achieving global convergence in bilevel optimization is known to be NP-hard (Vicente et al., 1994), and is only possible in finite time for those with linear or quadratic lower-level problems (Wang et al., 2021, 2022; Jeyakumar et al., 2016; Xiao and Chen, 2024). Unlike the aforementioned work, this paper considers a special bilevel problem BP and shows that this problem is solvable despite the persistent state-dependent bias.

3 Exact Solution under State-Dependent Bias via Bilevel Reformulation 

In this section, we first investigate the conditions under which Analog SGD converges exactly based on the insights from Theorem 1. When the condition is not satisfied, we propose a bilevel optimization problem whose optimal solutions coincide with the original ones despite the implicit penalty, and we develop a generalized hyper-gradient algorithm, which we call Residual Learning , to solve it.

Theorem 4 claims that Residual Learning converges in terms of both the upper and lower level objectives of problem BP . Compared with the classical μ-strongly convex benchmark, Residual Learning preserves the O (log K/K ) dependence up to logarithmic factors, which matches the rate of Digital SGD (Hazan and Kale, 2014; Karimi et al., 2016). Theorem 4 also suggests that the upper bound depends on two constants: κ1 and κ2.

Dependence on the objective condition number. In the classical convergence analysis of gradient-based optimization algorithms, κ1 := L/μ denotes the objective condition number , which characterizes the curvature of the objective landscape. If the objective is further twice differentiable, 

L and μ are the maximal and minimal eigenvalues of the objective’s Hessian. Agarwal et al. (2009) established the information-theoretic lower bound Ω  σ2/(μK ) for stochastic μ-strongly convex op-timization. Using Polyak-Ruppert averaging, Polyak and Juditsky (1992) showed that the averaged iterate f (ZK ) − f ∗ attains the optimal function value gap O  σ2/(μK ), where ZK denotes the weighted average of the iterates generated by Digital SGD . This lower bound depends explicitly on μ but not on 

κ1, indicating that the dependence on μ is fundamental, whereas the dependence on κ1 can be removed. However, the average iterate ZK introduces an additional variable to record the history of the iterates, which also suffers from state-dependent bias. Therefore, we consider only the last-iterate convergence in this work. Under this setting, Bottou et al. (2018) showed that Digital SGD attains that the last-iterate function value gap f (WK ) − f ∗ is bounded by a near-matching upper bound O  κ1σ2/(μK ), with an extra dependency on κ1 compared with the lower bound. Analogously, Theorem 4 introduces a linear factor in κ1, which is comparable to the classical upper bound. In light of the discussion above, the dependence on the objective condition number remains improvable via the Polyak-Ruppert averaging counterpart tailored for the biased hardware. We will investigate this in future work. 

Dependence on the hardware condition number. Similar to κ1, we investigate the hardware condition number κ2 := qmax /q min as the ratio of the maximum to the minimum response function. The bound in Theorem 4 reveals a significantly stronger dependence on the hardware condition number (κ42) compared to the objective condition number ( κ1), which implies that the convergence behavior is more sensitive to state-dependent bias induced by the hardware. A natural question arises: is this strong dependence on κ2 fundamental, or can it be improved by a more refined analysis? 6 Conclusions, Limitations, and Future Work 

This paper studies the convergence of gradient-based optimization under persistent state-dependent bias, motivated by analog in-memory computing. We prove that Analog SGD does not converge to the true minimizer of the original objective; instead, it converges to the minimizer of an implicitly penalized problem, and we characterize the resulting bias as a function of the hardware response model. To overcome this failure, we reformulate the training task as an equivalent bilevel optimization problem and propose Residual Learning , which provably achieves exact convergence to the solution of the original problem despite hardware imperfections. Beyond exact convergence, we establish that the convergence complexity of Residual Learning depends polynomially on the hardware condition number κ2, and we show via a hard-instance construction that this polynomial dependence is unavoidable in general. The theoretical results are corroborated by numerical simulations. One limitation of this work is the gap between the upper bound ˜O(κ42) and the lower bound ˜Ω(κ22) for Residual Learning ; it remains an open question whether a fundamental limitation exists for general stochastic algorithms on this class of hardware. Determining the sharp exponent for κ2 and developing an optimal algorithm with respect to 

κ2 remain open problems and constitute directions for future work.
